- **Status:** `observed` · Tier 4 · <https://arquivo.pt/noFrame/replay/20150816170410/https://brilliant.org/>

## Implications for the rebuild

- Restore breadth using the 2019-2024 legacy science catalog as the scope reference, not the 2026 one. Physics: everyday physics, mechanics, gravitation/orbits, E&M, waves/light, special relativity, quantum objects, astrophysics. Energy: solar PV and thermal. Chemistry: molecular representations, reactions/kinetics/equilibrium, plus the never-built 'chemical bond' course. Biology: genomics, molecular folding, computational biology. Electronics: circuits, digital circuits, logic circuitry. Engineering: structures, rockets/tethers. Information theory and Bayes, and quantitative finance. Use their topic decompositions only as syllabus skeletons and write all content fresh.
- Make breadth cheap with a small set of shared, parameterized simulation primitives rather than hand-built lessons. Retiring rather than rebuilding was plausibly a per-course content-cost problem (about 2x exercise density in rebuilt courses). Candidate primitives: circuit solver (Circuits, E&M, PV p-n junction, digital logic); N-body/orbit integrator (gravitation, astrophysics, Real-Engineering-style tethers); 2D ray optics (optics, waves); moments/statics solver (mobiles, structures, statics); gear trains; reaction energy-landscape and kinetics/equilibrium simulator (chemistry); molecule-representation viewer; sequence/RNA-folding (Nussinov) sandbox (biology); spin/Stern-Gerlach and qubit-circuit simulator (quantum); spacetime-diagram tool (relativity).
- Target 12-17 auto-checkable exercises per lesson (the rebuilt-course standard). Have a variation generator produce practice sets for every lesson so a science course is never 'thin' the way legacy courses (6-9 per lesson) and the 9-lesson E&M course were.
- Model content as a cross-domain prerequisite DAG at skill level, not just course level. Legacy pages already linked chemistry to biology to ML and physics to quantum. A knowledge-aware path engine can then route a user from, for example, Scientific Thinking to Molecules to Reactions to Computational Biology, and generate custom paths across subjects.
- Support several lesson and course types seen historically: typed lesson modes (Explore / Apply / Derive / Practice); a capstone applied 'Problem' lesson at the end of each level; themed phenomenon-first lessons (toilets, hurricanes, bridges); narrative thought-experiment games (laser tag, twin paradox); short 6-8 lesson 'mini-courses'; and a puzzle-a-day set (Science Puzzles style).
- Never silently remove content, since disappearing courses mid-progress is a top complaint. Version courses, keep any retired course readable in an Archived shelf on every device, migrate progress on rename, merge or split (as with Knowledge and Uncertainty becoming Bayesian Probability), and show a changelog.
- Bring back useful community-era mechanics in single-user form. A Daily Challenge with an 'insight' primer before solving. Several alternative explanations per problem (generated or user-written) that the user can rate and compare. User-authored problems and sets. A personal wiki/notes layer linked from exercises; a 2025 reviewer explicitly wanted better review tools and kept their own notes.
- Use the old 5-level difficulty and per-topic rating model as a placement and adaptivity seed: one probe at each of 5 difficulty levels per skill, then an Elo-style per-skill rating that drives knowledge-aware lesson and problem selection.
- Keep a read-only concept reference layer (wiki-like) separate from lessons. Brilliant kept its wiki as an archive because reference content outlives course rewrites; in the rebuild it can double as the Koji-style tutor's retrieval corpus.
